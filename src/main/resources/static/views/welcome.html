JUSTICE · FOR MY RIGHTS

Understand your options.

You deserve to be treated with dignity. Start by identifying what happened, what outcome you hope for, and which trusted local service can explain your rights.

A practical first step

Write down the key facts for yourself, including dates and any questions you want answered. If you need legal guidance or wish to raise a concern, contact a qualified local legal-aid or advocacy service. You can choose how much to share.

Care and fairness belong together.

TalkCare brings together approachable mental health and justice-support information. It is designed to help you consider your next step with clarity, compassion, and respect for your privacy.

TalkCare provides general information, not emergency support, professional mental health care, or legal advice. If someone is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services or a qualified crisis professional.

INDIVIDUAL · FOR MYSELF

Make room for yourself.

You do not have to have everything figured out. A small pause can help you notice what you need and decide on one manageable next step.

Try a gentle grounding moment

Feel your feet on the ground. Notice three things you can see, two things you can hear, and one thing you can feel. Breathe at a comfortable pace. If you would like more support, consider reaching out to a trusted person or qualified mental health professional.

PEERS · FOR OTHERS

Be there, without carrying it alone.

Showing up for someone can start with listening. Respect their choices, avoid pressure, and remember that your own well-being matters too.

A caring way to begin

Ask, “Would you like me to listen, help you find information, or sit with you?” Let them set the pace. If they may be in immediate danger, contact local emergency services or an appropriate crisis professional.
